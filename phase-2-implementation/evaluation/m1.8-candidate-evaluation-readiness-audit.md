## Business question and evidence levels

A customer requests ten units by 4 September. Eight fresh units can be supported on that date; a binding inbound source can support two by 8 September. A two-slice proposal can pass a **read-only, unreserved supply check** yet still require the customer's acceptance of the changed date and human deal review. The different illustrative 20-unit case (12 on the requested date; eight later from an uncommitted supplier offer) is not a binding candidate and cannot be counted as a verified success.

Successful execution, parse, authorized citation, proposal construction, fixed supply support, commercial disposition, reviewer usefulness and human approval are distinct outcomes. The fixed verifier is operational authority, but its output is not by itself independent ground truth for the claim that the AI improved a human decision.

- The 36 broad packets do not automatically yield M1.7 cases. Current AI eligibility requires an original recoverable shortage; the binding V3 contract further needs one eligible ungrouped finished-product line, partial permission, a complete returned binding path and two slices. Many packets instead have no shortage, an offer without binding receipt, a production/digital/service line or an independent policy issue. Check each packet against the *pinned* adapter before selection. Do not silently substitute the later Phase 1C draft reader or choose only cases on which the AI already did well.

**Review gate:** Create a reviewer-only case record bound to exact source/bundle hashes and business-rule version. A person who did not author the proposed answer or inspect model responses first reviews the submitted original, source facts, dated commitments, customer constraints, candidate feasibility and human action. They record identity, method, time, source pointers, expected acceptable outcomes and forbidden claims, then compare the pinned verifier and any author/model draft; disagreements are adjudicated and changes versioned. A reviewer can accept a case as useful for an *engineering* experiment even when broad Phase 1C release remains open, but must explicitly label that limited acceptance. The primary author or model drafts cannot self-certify independence. Keep the acceptance record and answer sheet outside agent inputs, tool responses and prompts. Separate approval is needed to designate an answer set as thesis ground truth.

## Proposed evaluation protocol and measurements

**Questions, stated before trials:** (Q1) Can eligible AI runs produce evidence-cited proposals that the pinned verifier supports, while maintaining pending customer/human action? (Q2) Does the boundary contain unsupported model claims and preserve the original result? (Q3) On the same cases, does AI add a useful, independently accepted option or explanation beyond Phase 1C's deterministic `discover_partial_delivery` / original-only decision, considering its cost and latency? (Q4) Does interruption/restart change any case outcome, budget or execution count? These are hypotheses, not findings.

1. **Freeze cases and answers:** Reserve separate development and evaluation case IDs/source states. Pin Phase 1C commit, Phase 2 commit, fixture/version, sealed bundle SHA-256 and source digest, as-of time, policies, customer permissions and eligibility result. Publish reviewer-only expectations and forbidden claims before model trials, with acceptance provenance. Do not pass an answer sheet, scenario intent, deterministic baseline output or reviewer notes to the model. Audit rendered prompt and authorized tool results for leakage.
2. **Offline/Pg first:** Use scripted model outputs to verify eligibility, V3 shape, exact returned references, raw rejected claim retention, verifier status, candidate/original separation, reviewer package and wrong-identity/changed-source/restart behavior. In a disposable PostgreSQL test database compare supported and refused candidates using the *exact* Phase 1C pin. Record per-case counts; these are engineering reliability checks, not AI success rates.
3. **Deterministic baseline:** On identical source context, run original fixed assessment plus the pinned `discover_partial_delivery`/recheck where eligible. Record no option, binding option, support status, unmet customer requirements and review package. Evaluate AI-only incremental candidate value/coverage and incremental tool/model latency and token use. If the deterministic discovery proposes the same acceptable candidate, credit AI only for independently rated explanation or other measured difference; do not claim discovery improvement. Do not infer multi-agent superiority over a single-agent approach; that would need a controlled comparator not proposed here.
4. **Bounded live pilot, separate approval:** After review, use Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b` with V3 binding opt-in on a small set of distinct eligible cases, sequentially, under existing 12 tool calls, 25 records, 30-day forward horizon, model-turn/token caps and confirmed free-tier access. Propose up to three repetitions per eligible case as a *pilot*; exact case count, repeat budget, free-tier quota and early-stop criteria require approval before running. Do not reset checkpoints between retries within a trial; use a new assessment/thread and same sealed source per independent repetition. Temperature 0 does not guarantee identical outputs. Never silently change model, prompt, source, limits or eligibility to fill a quota.
5. **Trial record:** Keep attempted/eligible/skipped counts by reason; model request/response IDs when available, provider/model/prompt/schema/configuration versions, timestamp, evidence hashes, source references actually returned, tools and arguments, output status, raw error/rejection and validated output, candidate identity, pinned verifier outcome, reviewer disposition, end-to-end and per-attempt latency, token usage, retry/budget events and stop reason. Record failures/abstentions and zero-output trials rather than dropping them. Sanitize keys and sensitive headers. Keep expected answers outside model-visible records.

| Metric (report denominator) | Definition and critical boundaries |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate generation and fixed support | Eligible runs; V3 proposal/abstention/finding/error; constructed proposals; proportion **of proposed candidates** passing fixed binding verification. Report customer acceptance and business route separately. Invalid or no proposal is not a fixed-verifier success. |
| Evidence/tool fidelity | Returned-reference precision, line/source/bundle match, authorized/valid tool arguments, calls per run and discovery-limit status; IDs existing somewhere in DB are insufficient. Invented binding supply/commitment, unauthorized source, false date or false citation are critical raw failures. |
| Containment | Count each raw model semantic error and its rejection/downgrade; separately count unsupported claims surviving in reviewer-visible fields. A critical survivor or unauthorized business approval blocks a safety-readiness claim even if other output is useful. |
| Appropriateness/usefulness | Independently reviewed acceptable proposal or abstention/finding; customer constraint and unmet-date clarity; matched comparison with deterministic baseline on the **same** case. Reviewer rates useful/neutral/misleading with rationale; do not equate fixed validity with customer value. |
| Reproducibility/resources | Repeated-case consistency and variance; restart/replay same final state, no extra completed model/tool/fixed-check trace; model attempts, latency distribution (including pacing), tokens per attempted and per valid candidate, rate limits/errors and free-tier usage. Distinguish provider latency from graph/database overhead. |

Analyze business cases, synthetic offline scenarios and fault injections separately, with paired case-level raw/validated/baseline outcomes and uncertainty counts. A zero observed critical failure in a tiny pilot is not evidence of a low population failure rate. A repeated run on one case is not another independent case. Predefine how human disagreements are adjudicated; retain rejected cases and failed trials in the denominator. Avoid selective post-hoc prompt tuning on the held-out cases; version any changes and start a new evaluation tranche.

**Threats to validity:** small synthetic case pool, reused sources/customer/catalogue, primary-author expectations, unresolved Phase 1C calibration and 0/36 accepted broad answers, deterministic baseline already able to discover binding splits, one provider/prompt, changing free-tier behavior, stochastic output at temperature zero, tool-selection ceiling, eligibility selection bias, reviewer subjectivity and human acceptance not observed. Checkpoint tests exercise selected interruption boundaries, not all possible crashes. A positive narrow binding pilot can support a *feasibility and safety-boundary* thesis finding, not a general win-rate claim.

**Estimated remaining work before a meaningful narrow AI-candidate conclusion:** at least case authoring and source freeze, independent review/adjudication, paired baseline/harness, green offline/PostgreSQL tests, and a separately approved small live Groq trial with analysis—roughly five dependency gates, with human review and genuinely distinct eligible source states on the critical path. The existing 72 passing tests complete none of the independent live-candidate scoring. Generalizable AI accuracy or a multi-agent/single-agent performance comparison would require a larger accepted set and a separately designed controlled study. M1.8 implementation, any Phase 1C pin change and any live API use remain unapproved by this audit.
