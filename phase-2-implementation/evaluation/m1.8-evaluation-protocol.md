# M1.8 binding-candidate evaluation protocol (implementation stage)

**Scope and authority.** Phase 2 `phase2-agent-implementation`, exact separate Phase 1C SHA `90c0bc8e285ee69262c7db826b786096a77ad004`. This implementation prepares cases, comparator, capture and review; **no M1.8 live Groq trials have run and no case has been accepted by a human reviewer**. Business authority is the pinned Phase 1C reader and `verify_candidate`; the graph manages original/candidate isolation, bounded source access and PostgreSQL recovery. AI proposals are hypotheses; a supported, unreserved two-slice option still needs customer acceptance and human review. The 20-unit 12+8 conditional supplier-offer illustration cannot be verified by this binding-only contract.

## Experimental questions and predeclared hypotheses

1. For an eligible shortage with binding dated supply, can a model investigate authorized records and produce an evidence-grounded V3 proposal supported by the pinned fixed verifier, or correctly abstain? This is unmeasured until a live trial.
2. Do unsupported quantities, dates, citations and commitments remain rejected or nonactionable, with raw rejections retained and no unsupported business claim in reviewer output? Engineering fault tests check containment; independent semantic review is required for real model responses.
3. Does any model contribution provide useful information **beyond** the pinned rule-only `discover_partial_delivery` on the identical sealed evidence? A matching split demonstrates no discovery improvement. A separate blinded human usefulness rating of a supported distinct option or explanation is required; do not infer superiority from parsing or verifier acceptance.
4. Does restart/repeated execution preserve original and candidate identities, evidence, completed work, tool budget and outcome? Existing M1.7 checkpoint tests are operational controls, not independent business scenarios.

## Cohorts and selection

`backend/tests/m18_case_setup.py` creates five proposed eligible binding source states B01–B05 and four distinct negative controls N01–N04 only in a **disposable Phase 2 CI PostgreSQL business database**. They share the pinned reference's commercial/master attributes but use distinct customer/product/source/location/deal/run identities. The source facts vary early allocation, later quantity/arrival, request date and transit. The controls vary partial permission, cancelled inbound, stale stock and absent supply. Source generation is intentionally not a replacement rules engine; the exact pinned reader determines all options and the exact pinned verifier checks a candidate. Cross-case isolation and bundle hashes are tested. Fixture diversity is limited: one product class, region, price/policy family and one source-pattern family. Cases failing the pinned eligibility or recheck gate must be reported as exclusions or corrected transparently before any pilot, not silently counted as successes.

The [source-first sheet](m1.8-case-review-sheet.md) is prepared before model output. It lists the submitted deal, dated source facts, provisional original/baseline and acceptable/forbidden outcomes, customer acceptance and unresolved limitations. B01 resembles the positive M1.7 integration test, but its sources are new disposable IDs, not a new externally accepted business answer. N controls are gate/safety trials, not AI-candidate successes. Other malformed JSON, wrong-source references, hash tampering and checkpoint restart are fault injections, not business cases. Never combine their denominators or count repetitions as new cases.

## Comparison and data-flow boundary

On each identical pinned bundle, `pinned_baseline` calls the pinned Phase 1C `discover_partial_delivery` directly, then calls the Phase 2 read-only adapter's `verify_candidate`, which re-reads original/candidate in one repeatable-read transaction and compares source digests. The baseline result records no-proposal reason or exact slices/source IDs, fixed supply status and aggregate business status. If original/customer date is unmet, even a fixed-supported baseline remains revision-needed. Do not pass this output to the model.

`capture_trial` records a completed graph checkpoint's case/run/deal/bundle/digest identity, provider/model/prompt label, original review status, execution/contribution/error status, raw rejected-claim audit, candidate verification/rejection, fixed supply/business outcomes, reviewer package, returned tool references and bounded arguments, model reservations/outcomes, usage and latency if available, trace and stopping reason. It rejects mismatched identities and is **not** a semantic answer judge. It retains raw rejected claims separately from the reviewer package; reviewer-visible semantic errors require human judgment. `structural_flags` recognizes a fixed-supported option, checks candidate citations against returned references and identifies a matching quantity/date baseline schedule, but always flags distinct value and semantic correctness for review. `adjudicate` requires answer identity and one of `developer_provisional`, `developer_supervisor_accepted`, `independent_accepted`; accepted labels need a dated reviewer/source-check record, and independent acceptance additionally needs an independence attestation. The helper does not itself infer business correctness or prove the reviewer was independent. Proposed human review records never enter `FulfillmentInputV1`, graph state used by a model, prompts, permitted tools or source pool. Routine CI exercises only scripted doubles and fixed code, with no API credentials.

## Reviewer and scoring gate

Before a live pilot, Ali may review the source rows, sealed reader facts, as-of/dated commitments, original deterministic finding, baseline proposal/recheck, partial permission, exact candidate feasibility and reviewer action. Record his per-case acceptance, correction, rejection or deferral as **developer/supervisor-side human review** with source hashes and date; it is not independent external ground truth. Another reviewer who did not author the expectations or inspect model output can check a subset and record disagreements/resolution for stronger thesis evidence. Source hashes prove provenance, not business correctness. A developer-authored assertion that passed CI remains **developer-provisional** until Ali's decision. A case can be rejected/deferred; never turn it into accepted ground truth merely to fill the pilot denominator. Record a versioned correction before trial, preserving earlier answer history.

For accepted cases, report case-level eligible/skipped/attempted/proposed/abstained/error counts; returned-citation precision and unauthorized attempts; candidate construction versus fixed `supply_supported_unreserved` versus aggregate `needs_revision`; reviewer usefulness relative to baseline; raw semantic failures versus those surviving in reviewer-visible content; model attempts, per-attempt and end-to-end latency, tokens and tool calls. A fabricated binding source, unsupported confirmed date/commitment, unauthorized or invented citation, and false approval-ready promise are critical regardless of a useful-looking proposal. A rejected raw failure remains counted as a raw failure even when containment works. Human raters mark correct/incorrect/uncertain with evidence and rationale; no broad accuracy score from mixed source types or tiny samples. Include negative results, exclusions, rate limits and repeated-execution inconsistencies.

Keep case/answer files in the evaluation/test paths, away from the production runtime imports. Blind the reviewer to model identity for comparative semantic rating where practical; baseline and model outputs must share source context, but the baseline's existence should not be hidden in the final paired analysis. A model does not receive answers, baseline output or reviewer corrections. Version Phase 2 commit, pin, fixture code, schema/policy, sealed hash, run ID, prompt, provider/model parameters and time for each trial. A new assessment/thread is required for an independent repetition; resuming one thread is an idempotency check, not another sample.

## Reproducibility, current results and threats

CI's existing two-checkout workflow creates separate disposable business/checkpoint PostgreSQL databases, applies the pinned schema/reference rows/compiled rules and installs locked Python dependencies. The tests install M1.8 rows only in that disposable database and call the pinned reader/discovery/verifier without business writes by the adapter. Run `PYTHONPATH=phase-2-implementation/backend python -m unittest discover -s phase-2-implementation/backend/tests -p 'test_*.py' -v` with the CI environment variables. [CI 39590252565](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39590252565) passed 78 tests without skips and printed each sealed hash, source digest and verified baseline; [CI 39661859652](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39661859652) passed 80, adding scripted graph capture and a reopened PostgreSQL checkpointer/repeated-execution comparison. The [final code CI 39662789074](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39662789074) passed 81 tests with no skips, including the explicit review-status contract, raw/reviewer outcome recording and pinned original findings. The [review sheet](m1.8-case-review-sheet.md) keeps observed results beside the prior source-first expectations. Local execution in the later coding environment lacks the locked LangGraph package and PostgreSQL service, so it does not replace CI validation.

The initial fixture loader failed twice in CI before the successful runs: the pinned inventory trigger requires `deal_desk` in the fixture transaction's search path, and submitted deals forbid adding lines. The Phase 2 disposable loader now sets a transaction-local search path and constructs the line while its deal is Draft, then submits it. Neither fix changed Phase 1C. Preserve the failed runs [39589570944](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39589570944) and [39589911118](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39589911118) in the engineering trace; they are setup failures, not model-quality outcomes.

Threats: synthetic, developer-authored answer states; no independent acceptance; one provider later; narrow single-line, stocked-product and two-slice scope; correlated common source pattern/policy; deterministic discovery already proposes the obvious binding split; model output stochasticity; eligibility selection; small N and unmeasured human utility. Even zero critical survivors in a small pilot is not a population reliability estimate. No single-agent or broad multi-agent superiority conclusion is justified without a controlled comparator.
