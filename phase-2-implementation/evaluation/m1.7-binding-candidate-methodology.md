# M1.7 binding candidate evaluation and handoff

**Scope approved by Ali:** binding-only candidate construction, pinned Phase 1C fixed verification, original/candidate isolation, full six-responsibility candidate mapping and typed human handoff. The conditional 20-unit 12+8 supplier-offer schedule is a distinct **unverified** scenario. M1.6 nonactionable ideas do not enter this candidate workflow. Groq remains the provisional development provider; routine M1.7 tests use an offline model double and make no paid/live requests.

## Authority and scenario categories

The salesperson's original submission, Phase 1C read-only reader and fixed aggregate decision are authoritative. An AI proposal is a hypothesis even if it cites a returned binding option. The Orchestrator assigns candidate identity and Phase 1C alone decides cumulative dated supply, permissions and business rules. `supply_supported_unreserved` is not customer acceptance, stock reservation, delivery promise or business approval. Reviewer actions remain human.

The answer expectations above are source-first assertions, authored outside prompts and provider requests. They are not accepted ground-truth labels for broad model quality. A future independently reviewed answer set is needed to estimate semantic correctness across many deals.

## Execution and measurements

The opt-in M1.7 model output uses a versioned, two-slice payload: line ID, whole-unit quantity, ISO delivery date, per-slice source references and fixed tradeoff code. No model-provided candidate ID, approval, commitment status or executable business operation is trusted. The validator checks identity and source references against the append-only completed tool ledger and full returned `inspect_fulfillment_paths` binding options; it does **not** reimplement dated availability. The trusted Orchestrator constructs a Phase 1C `Candidate` and calls its existing read-only `verify_candidate` adapter; a changed seal or source digest stops verification.

The same LangGraph state retains the immutable original fixed results and a separate candidate record. Configuration maps first, followed sequentially by Pricing, Fulfillment, Credit, Policy, reconciliation and Approval Routing for the candidate. All six are distinct **mappings of one Phase 1C aggregate fixed decision**, not six independent engines. The candidate's own subject ID is checked on each result. PostgreSQL checkpoints preserve source identity, tool budget, proposal lineage and completed nodes. On restart, read-only candidate verification may safely rerun against the exact pinned seal if it had not checkpointed its output; it cannot refresh changed evidence or duplicate a business write.

### Checkpoint recovery repair

The first PostgreSQL CI after the candidate graph was added failed because a resumed review update attributed to `finish` evaluated its conditional outgoing edge again and scheduled `candidate_construct` despite an existing candidate. The transition now requires `candidate_status` to be `not_started` before scheduling construction. A subsequent CI exposed a second missing edge: after successful candidate Approval Routing the graph reached END without entering `candidate_review`, so the wrapper produced an original-only package. Candidate Approval Routing now always transitions to candidate review. These are graph-control defects, not changes to Phase 1C supply rules.

The restart regression interrupts after construction, closes its PostgreSQL checkpointer, resumes with a new checkpointer, and compares the pinned context, original six results, claim audit, candidate identity, tool-call budget and completed-node trace. It calls the completed workflow a second time to detect duplicate verification, model/tool work or package changes. The reviewer package labels supported supply **unreserved** and customer acceptance **pending**, includes the candidate's own six fixed findings, and cites source projections returned by the authorized tool. An unsupported fixed candidate remains a recorded check with a `not_confirmed_by_fixed_verifier` interpretation and withheld route. The original and candidate outcomes must never overwrite one another.

Record separately: total/offline/PostgreSQL test counts, successful binding fixed checks, conditional/unsupported paths rejected, raw AI proposal rejections, surviving reviewer-facing unsupported claims (critical), checkpoint field size, tool calls before/after restart and whether original results changed. CI output is the source for measured numbers. No live Groq quality or token/latency result is claimed for M1.7; the opt-in model schema has offline tests only until a separately bounded live exercise is approved.

The direct pinned adapter test uses a transaction-local 10-unit deal: eight units from returned stock on the requested date and two from an actual confirmed inbound source on the later date. Phase 1C reports `supply_supported_unreserved` for the candidate and `needs_revision` for the aggregate business decision. The latter preserves pending customer acceptance. The candidate reviewer package displays both original and candidate specialist findings, exact source references and selected returned source fields, an explicit unreserved verification outcome, pending acceptance and a withheld ordinary route. The source rows, original deal and original six mapped findings remain unchanged. This test demonstrates the narrow binding check; it does not independently establish the model's ability to generate the proposal in production.

The adverse cases check an unsupported later date; a conditional supplier offer masquerading as binding; unseen or wrong-line citations; customer partial-delivery denial; zero, fractional and malformed quantities/dates; run, subject and seal mismatches; incomplete or stale source projections; tool authorization and call-budget replay; and malformed model contributions. For a dated offer idea, its original rejected claim remains in the audit trace. The reviewer package may carry only the validated, explicitly `downgraded_nonactionable` inquiry and omits the rejected date. Technical errors and verification failures do not change the original Phase 1C assessment. Existing M1.0–M1.6 regressions test the lower-level evidence boundaries and offline fallback; the M1.7 PostgreSQL test crosses the candidate node/checkpoint boundary.

The completed candidate checkpoint field measured **9,973 bytes** in the first passing PostgreSQL CI run at commit `552c514ad2c6c337592aee2e064e2fd6886953e9`. This is `pg_column_size(checkpoint)` for one completed test thread, not total relation storage, database growth per run, latency or a deployment capacity estimate. That run passed 71 tests with no skips in CI. A further offline handoff regression for conditional inquiries was added afterward; its final CI result must be reported separately.

## Limitations and follow-up gates

- One single eligible stocked or supplier-finished line, two slices, and at most one candidate in this implementation; W1's two-proposal and one-revision ceilings remain upper bounds, not a reason to implement an unnecessary loop now.
- The fixed Phase 1C verifier accepts binding shipping options at one location and returns unreserved support. Mixed binding/offer, substitution, production, broad multi-line and selective rerun are not validated by M1.7.
- The `inspect_fulfillment_paths` tool marks a pool containing both binding options and supplier offers as `unsupported_capability`. M1.7 refuses to promote a binding candidate from that incomplete mixed-path classification even if some individual binding source is visible.
- Phase 1C's blocked-route interface may not provide required human role names; the reviewer package marks these unavailable rather than inferring them.
- Phase 1C candidate test deal rows are transaction-local or disposable synthetic setup. Tests do not establish independently reviewed thesis answer labels, broad AI performance, or safe replay after the source database materially changes.
