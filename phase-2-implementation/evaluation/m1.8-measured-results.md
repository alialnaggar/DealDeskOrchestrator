## Source preparation and baseline

`backend/tests/m18_case_setup.py` loads nine distinct disposable Phase 2 source states against the pinned Phase 1C schema in CI's business database. Each has a separate run, original deal, customer, product, location, lane and stock/inbound IDs. Five permitted binding shortages vary quantity, early stock, confirmed inbound date and quantity, request date and transit. Four negative controls isolate permission denial, cancelled receipt, stale stock without later receipt and no selected supply. These are synthetic development cases derived from the pinned reference's master/commercial attributes, not Phase 1C released data or representative field frequencies. The case loader writes only to the disposable test database; the Phase 2 adapter performs repeatable-read, read-only business transactions.

`pinned_baseline` uses the exact pinned `phase1c_partial_discovery.discover_partial_delivery` on the sealed bundle and calls the existing `Phase1CAdapter.verify_candidate` if it returns a candidate. The adapter checks the re-read source identity and digest in a repeatable-read transaction. It records the original fixed business and Fulfillment status, baseline candidate quantities/dates/source IDs, fixed candidate supply result and aggregate business status. No Phase 2 availability rule or synthetic expected answer determines candidate validity. See the [source-first review sheet](m1.8-case-review-sheet.md) for full sealed bundle hashes, input digests and each case's original and baseline outcomes.

## Harness and regression evidence

`backend/dealdesk_phase2/evaluation_m18.py` checks matching run/deal/evidence identities, captures the original and candidate outcomes and keeps model errors, failed/reserved attempts, raw rejected claims, reviewer-visible package, returned tool sources/arguments, candidate rejection, timestamps, latency and usage where available. Mechanical flags check fixed support, returned candidate citations and schedule equivalence to the baseline. Business correctness, critical surviving claims and usefulness are reserved for human trial judgment; a structured answer alone is not a correctness score. An accepted answer requires a dated reviewer/source record, with `developer_supervisor_accepted` kept separate from `independent_accepted`. The sheet and acceptance records are not in the model's restricted inputs or tools.

## Four-trial live Groq pilot — measured, not a quality generalization

On 21 October 2026, Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b` was listed on its free tier
(30 RPM, 1,000 RPD, 8,000 TPM, 200,000 TPD in the published model table).
The opt-in Actions job required the existing repository free-tier attestation
and a present secret without displaying either; its authenticated model-list
GET confirmed the exact ID before every generation. No billing was enabled,
provider switched, hidden retry made or extra trial run. The source and
configuration were locked to Phase 1C SHA above, Phase 2 pilot code
`f2502eee379b139510e822e60997068c38f6002e`, V3 prompt
`m16-fulfillment-claims-v1-m17-binding-optin`, temperature 0, 1,024 decision
and 2,048 final output-token caps, sequential requests at least 12 seconds
apart, 12 tool calls maximum, 25 records/response and 30-day forward horizon.
The output is [four preserved sanitized observations](m1.8-live-observations.json),
including exact case/run/source hashes, prompt digests, attempts, usage, raw
rejection, reviewer package and trace. The answers and deterministic baseline
were absent from the model's input. B01 and B05 belong to one related
synthetic stocked-product case family; two repeats are not new cases.

All four model responses used `inspect_fulfillment_paths` once and referenced
source IDs returned by that authorized call. Because the proposals failed the
whole-unit type/format gate first, their source-to-slice correlation and exact
schedule were **not** verified in the live workflow. No rejected raw text,
unsupported commitment, candidate, reservation or approval appeared in the
reviewer package. Each raw proposal and its rejection reason remain in the
audit. The Actions jobs succeeded in capturing the experiment, while **all
four AI candidate attempts failed**. Zero live AI candidates passed Phase 1C
verification. No HTTP, quota or authorization errors were observed in the 12
recorded model requests. Total recorded usage was 13,772 input and 11,495
output tokens, four counted tool calls and a mean 28,692 ms end-to-end per
trial. These are API token counts, not monetary charges.

The fixed discovery baseline already returns B01 8 Sep 4 + 2 Sep 8 and B05
11 Sep 4 + 7 Sep 11, both `supply_supported_unreserved` with aggregate
`needs_revision`. **A different early date is not automatically invalid.**
After the pilot, a separately labeled deterministic diagnostic normalized
only the whole-unit quantity syntax and checked B01 8 Sep 3 + 2 Sep 8 and B05
11 Sep 2 + 7 Sep 11 (each later slice citing its inbound source). The exact
pinned verifier reported `supply_supported_unreserved` and aggregate
`needs_revision` for both in [post-pilot CI](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39666810812).
This is an exploratory **counterfactual**: it is neither a verified live model
candidate nor proof of customer acceptance or added utility. B01/1's extra
stock reference in its later slice was not the diagnostic's input. The finding
shows that deterministic discovery's chosen split is not a complete list of
every structurally supported schedule, but it does not establish that the
AI delivered a usable improvement in any live run.

The initial capture flag `candidate_citations_returned=true` was vacuously
true when no candidate existed. It was **not counted as citation success**;
the subsequent harness correction reports this as `null` (unobserved) and has
an offline regression. Never conflate a returned source ID with a verified
source-to-slice claim. The four artifacts preserve the exact historical flag
and this correction rather than silently rewriting measurements.

The full [post-pilot PostgreSQL regression run 39666810812](https://github.com/alialnaggar/DealDeskOrchestrator/actions/runs/39666810812)
passed **83 tests without skips**, including the exploratory fixed-date probe,
original/candidate isolation, prior interruption/restart, evidence identity,
tool permissions, and the corrected absent-candidate citation flag. The pilot
did not itself interrupt live calls or empirically test interruption under
the provider. Each trial used a separate assessment/thread; the two cases
retained their exact sealed hashes and source digests. The live branch has
four deliberate pushes and no automated routine/push-to-Phase2 live trigger.

**Interpretation and threats.** This bounded pilot found a repeatable output
contract problem in the model's V3 binding proposals. The strict boundary
contained it, preserving the original assessment. It did not measure valid
live candidate quality or realized value beyond baseline. The normalized
early-date diagnostic suggests a testable later hypothesis, not a reported AI
success. Cases are synthetic, related, developer/supervisor accepted rather
than externally independent, and selected through an eligibility gate. Four
trials provide no broad accuracy or reliability estimate, no independent
single-agent comparator, and no multi-agent superiority evidence. The
20-unit conditional 12+8 supplier offer remains outside this binding-only
contract. A second blind human reviewer on a subset would strengthen thesis
ground truth. Further live calls and the next milestone require separate
authorization.

Remaining threats: small synthetic family with correlated commercial/policy defaults, provisional author expectations, unreviewed Phase 1C broad answers (0/36 accepted at the audited checkpoint), eligibility selection, a deterministic discovery baseline already solving the obvious splits, unmeasured customer utility, and no live V3 candidate outcomes. Do not report an AI accuracy percentage, AI advantage over the baseline, or multi-agent superiority from this stage.
