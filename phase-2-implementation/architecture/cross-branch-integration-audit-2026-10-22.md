## What the live Phase 1C branch actually provides

The current `phase-1c-data/implementation/phase1c_reader.py` selects dated rows for a run; `phase1c_policy_decision.py` and `phase1c_deal_decision.py` compute compiled commercial findings and the conservative whole-deal gate. The 24-table PostgreSQL schema, 14 isolated reference cases, 100 provisional development regressions and 36 versioned v2 evaluation inputs have separate roles. The 36 inputs are reproducibly pinned, but **0/36 independently accepted expected answers**; the current human disposition intake has 36 pending. `ready_for_generation=false`. The 400 historical submissions are diagnostics, not labels. See `phase-1c-data/current-status.md`, `implementation/phase1c_data_completion_plan.md`, `implementation/phase1c_release_review.md`, `evaluation/reviews/v2_review_handoff.md` and `implementation/phase1c_evaluation_acceptance.py`. The latest append-only completion-plan entries supersede older counts: scoped source-data/calibration dispositions cover the bounded fictional V1 conventions; `agent_execution_log.output_json` remains an open runtime-output field mark. Historical supplier calendars, market calibration and case-level review retain their documented limits. Case 020's modal route is a selected *proposed interpretation*, not an accepted six-responsibility answer.

`phase1c_candidate.py` defines a typed original-linked candidate and source digest. A partial-delivery reader recheck is implemented only for a narrow stocked/supplier-finished binding-supply path at a common location, with quantity/date/customer-constraint checks and no reservation. A `substitute` proposal shape exists, but that is not a demonstrated full substitution verifier. `phase1c_partial_discovery.py` supplies a bounded deterministic partial-delivery baseline. `phase1c_candidate_batch.py` can compare one or two distinct candidates in one caller-owned read-only repeatable-read transaction, without selecting one. `phase1c_candidate_trace.py` builds/replays hashed sidecar traces, an experiment rather than durable access-controlled storage. Conditional supplier offers are not binding receipts and cannot establish the illustrative 12+8 commitment. The binding candidate's aggregate can remain `needs_revision` because changing the requested schedule still needs customer acceptance.

**Newer development runtime, not an agent runtime:** `phase1c_development_runtime.py` runs the real reader and aggregate deterministic decision, maps them into six responsibility summaries plus an Orchestrator gate, validates seven output envelopes, and persists them to `deal_desk.agent_execution_log` and `deal_runs`. `validate_phase1c_runtime_outputs.py` checks envelope shape, vocabulary, duplicate attempt identity and citations against selected reader IDs; it does not prove business correctness or that an AI tool returned a record. `persist_development_attempt` commits either seven success rows or, following rollback, one failed Orchestrator row with the exception class and frozen identity. It rejects repeat execution; it has no automatic retry, per-specialist AI calls, candidate lifecycle or crash resume. Tests exercise physical/digital examples, bad references, committed failure and competing claims on one run. See `implementation/phase1c_runtime_output_review.md` and `test_phase1c_development_runtime.py`. Its status words (`Success` execution versus `supported/conditional/unknown/infeasible/blocked` business finding) must not be confused with final deal approval.

Comparison from pin to live branch: the core `phase1c_candidate.py`, `phase1c_candidate_batch.py`, `phase1c_candidate_trace.py`, `phase1c_deal_decision.py` and `phase1c_partial_discovery.py` have no file changes; `phase1c_reader.py` and `phase1c_development_runtime.py` changed, and the human disposition intake was added. This is a source comparison, not a compatibility-test result.

## What Phase 2 has implemented and where it overlaps

`backend/dealdesk_phase2/phase1c_adapter.py` imports only the **pinned** reader, candidate and aggregate decision. It refuses a different checkout HEAD or changed tracked implementation; its reads use read-only repeatable-read transactions. `evidence_bundle.py` seals a reversible canonical reader bundle and `graph_state.py` carries run/deal/as-of/config/policy/catalogue/source and bundle identity. `orchestrator.py` maps one pinned aggregate into sequential Configuration, Pricing, Fulfillment, Credit, Policy, reconciliation and Approval Routing nodes through `fixed_specialists.py`. The six results are **deterministic responsibility views of one Phase 1C decision**, not six independent AI reasoning engines. A separate Groq-backed Fulfillment investigation (`investigation_graph.py`, `live_models.py`) is the only implemented AI specialist behavior, governed by three read-only tools, a default-deny scope, tool/model attempt ledgers, typed claim validation and conditional eligibility. Gemini remains optional, not a measured reasoning winner.

`candidate_graph.py` constructs at most one V3 binding proposal from the AI result, invokes the pinned Phase 1C reader/verifier, maps all six deterministic candidate results separately from the original, and prepares a human package. M1.6 rejected claims remain in audit; unverified ideas cannot be promoted silently. `checkpoint.py` uses an isolated PostgreSQL LangGraph checkpointer and tests interruption/restart and no unnecessary completed-node replay. `evaluation_m18.py` captures raw and reviewer-visible errors, baseline comparison, source digests, tokens/latency/tool calls and candidate outcomes. Five synthetic eligible cases and four controls are developer/supervisor accepted, not externally independent. Four bounded live Groq trials on two related cases all failed strict quantity-format validation *before* candidate verification; none established realized AI improvement. See `evaluation/m1.8-measured-results.md` and the approved roadmap.

Overlap is deliberate at the **deterministic decision and six-domain presentation** boundary. Phase 1C's new seven-row development log and Phase 2's six LangGraph results should not both claim orchestration authority or be written as duplicate official executions. Phase 2 currently does not call the new Phase 1C runtime, batch comparison, sidecar trace or 36-case acceptance intake. New code at the live Phase 1C head is **not** evidence that the pinned adapter already supports its changed reader outputs.

## One deal through both branches

For a customer requesting **20 units by Friday**, with **12 binding units** supportable by Friday, a possible later **eight-unit supplier offer** and permission for partial delivery: Phase 1C's dated reader and fixed gate can establish the 12 supported units and label the offer conditional. It does **not** verify a 12+8 delivery commitment. Phase 2 seals that source view, runs Configuration first, retains all six fixed responsibility findings, and can ask Fulfillment AI to inspect authorized records and explain the conditional option. A conditional planning claim stays unverified in the reviewer package; the current binding-only candidate verifier must reject its promotion. A human must obtain supplier confirmation and customer acceptance before a new checked context or revised submission. Neither runtime approves the deal or reserves stock.
