## 1. Product, thesis question and authority

A salesperson submits a structured deal: product and configuration, line quantities and prices, requested arrival, destination, commercial terms, customer constraints and supporting material. The system gathers dated evidence, applies fixed rules, investigates recoverable gaps and prepares a traceable package for human reviewers. The thesis tests both business decision correctness and orchestration behavior: evidence grounding, failures, retries, handoff and reproducibility. A meaningful AI contribution must investigate authorized facts, explain tradeoffs or suggest a bounded alternative; repeating deterministic discovery alone is not demonstrated improvement.

**Example used throughout:** the customer requests 20 units by Friday, 12 are supportable on that date and eight might arrive later if a supplier confirms. Partial delivery is permitted. The later eight are conditional; the pinned Phase 1C verifier does not establish the 12+8 mixed conditional schedule as a binding delivery commitment. It can be an explicitly unverified planning idea. A different, fully binding two-slice proposal can enter the current fixed candidate verifier, which may label supply supported but unreserved and the whole deal still needing revision and customer acceptance.

The salesperson's submission is immutable. Neither the AI nor the LangGraph Orchestrator approves a deal, waives policy, reserves inventory, purchases supply, books capacity, promises delivery or edits the submission. AI output is a hypothesis. Phase 1C's deterministic reader, rules and verifier decide hard quantities, dates, source commitment and fixed statuses. A human decides what to do with the reviewed original and any candidate. Execution success, fixed business support and human approval are three separate states.

## 4. Source identity and run lifecycle

1. **Start:** trusted caller assigns assessment/thread ID and Phase 1C run ID; validate the original deal, as-of timestamp, catalogue, policy and configuration identity. Never trust model-generated identifiers.
2. **Pin:** `Phase1CAdapter.read_original` opens a read-only repeatable-read transaction in the pinned checkout and serializes the selected reader output canonically (`evidence_bundle.py`); state carries `source_input_digest` and `bundle_sha256`. The as-of cutoff selects dated facts, the transaction snapshot selects visible rows for the read, and the digest identifies the sealed result. These are different concepts. A later verification rereads both original and candidate within one read-only repeatable-read transaction and rejects a changed selected source or mismatch instead of silently refreshing it.
3. **Fixed original:** Configuration first obtains the one authoritative Phase 1C aggregate decision; typed nodes map the six responsibility views. Post-node identity/producer/version checks and explicit prerequisite gates run. Reconciliation retains blockers and unknowns; routing is withheld when required checks are incomplete.
4. **AI eligibility:** a narrow fixed shortfall with valid prerequisites may trigger Fulfillment; no AI call for an irrelevant or unsafe original. AI may abstain, report a bounded finding or suggest an unverified proposal. Model attempt reservations and outcomes are checkpointed. A completed attempt can replay; an interrupted in-flight request is not silently repeated.
5. **Investigation:** model requests one of three named tools. The adapter checks the trusted scope and pinned context, enforces at most 12 calls, 25 source records per response and a 30-day *forward* search horizon without hiding relevant historical provenance. Append the result and actually returned IDs to the checkpointed access ledger; reject an invented citation even when an ID exists elsewhere in the database. Tool response states distinguish no eligible evidence, conditional/insufficient evidence, unsupported capability, denial and technical failure.
6. **Claims and candidates:** typed M1.6 claims are source observation, fixed finding reference, conditional possibility, evidence gap or candidate idea. `claim_validation.py` records accepted and rejected claims with raw text/reason in the audit; reviewer summaries use validated claims and fixed findings. M1.7 V3 `binding_proposal` is opt-in, uses exact positive ASCII whole-unit quantity strings, exact delivery dates and source-to-slice references. Runtime assigns candidate ID and lineage; it cannot upgrade an M1.6 nonactionable idea behind the scenes.
7. **Verify and compare:** `candidate_graph.py` constructs at most one M1.7 binding candidate and invokes `Phase1CAdapter.verify_candidate`. Fixed Phase 1C rereads correlated evidence, verifies candidate and reassembles the whole decision; Phase 2 maps all six results again, reconciles and recomputes routing. Rejected or mismatched candidates retain failure status and audit while original results remain untouched. `supply_supported_unreserved` is not reservation or customer consent.
8. **Handoff:** reviewer package keeps original and candidate results, exact changes, evidence provenance, unresolved requirements, accepted and rejected model claims, routing status and stopping reason. A separate authorized business workflow would create any revised submission after human action; it does not exist here.

The checkpoint stores sealed bundle data for the bounded M1 case, not a live PostgreSQL transaction spanning graph nodes. M1.0 measured a roughly 38 KB sealed bundle; checkpoint storage and restart were measured in M1.1 and later. A new Phase 1C reader version that changes bundle shape requires a newly identified assessment context. The M1.9 measured empty `component_supplier_offers` addition is a concrete example.

## 5. Contracts, tool authority and errors

`contracts.py`, `graph_state.py`, `fulfillment_agent.py`, `candidate_contract.py` and `tool_permissions.py` provide the current Python/JSON contracts. The graph's `AssessmentStateV1` includes run/context IDs, sealed evidence, fixed decision digest, per-subject specialist results, trace, tool/model ledgers, claim audit, candidate records and reviewer package. Specialist results have a version, trusted specialist and deterministic producer, assessment/subject/bundle IDs, execution, finding, prerequisite, payload and error. The AI result uses separate contribution/abstention/error status; producer and verification status are supplied by trusted runtime. The current code is a bounded implementation of the broader W3 common-envelope design, not proof that every planned AI specialist output type exists.

Current tool registry enables only Fulfillment: `inspect_line_supply`, `inspect_fulfillment_paths` and `inspect_evidence_record`. The other five allowlists are empty. `TrustedToolScope` binds specialist, subject, customer, line, source category and `RunContextV1`. Investigation reads the sealed selected pool; its bounded absence is not a global assertion that no supply exists. The source-first reviewer answer sheets are never passed to the model. Authorized tool response references and dated facts remain distinct from the model's interpretation.

| Condition | Safe control behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Business blocker | Record fixed finding, run independent eligible checks, withhold ordinary routing when required. Do not retry to obtain a different business answer. |
| Conditional or missing evidence | Show exact gap/condition and source scope; never promote to confirmed supply. Missing capability is labeled separately. |
| Abstention | Completed reasoning outcome, no candidate; preserve original fixed assessment. |
| Model malformed/timeout/quota | Technical error and checkpointed attempt; original fixed assessment survives; no favorable default. |
| Unauthorized call or fabricated citation | Deny/reject and audit without leaking unauthorized source data. |
| Candidate invalid/source mismatch | Reject at exact fixed verification boundary; no unchecked candidate shown as supported. Digest mismatch stops affected verification. |
| Interruption/replay | Resume checkpoint with same assessment, source identity and budgets; do not repeat completed fixed candidate verification or completed model response merely due to recovery. |

M1 proposal limits of two, one revision and one transient retry remain provisional design defaults; M1.7 currently supports only one candidate, no search loop. Do not document a two-candidate production search as implemented. No new library, queue, vector store, tracing SaaS or paid provider is authorized by this blueprint.

### Required data fields when adding a specialist

## 11. Evaluation design to defend in the thesis

**Research questions to test, not presumed results:** Does a specialist produce source-grounded, useful alternatives beyond the pinned deterministic discovery baseline on matched cases? Do the typed validation and fixed verifier prevent unsupported model claims from becoming reviewer-facing business support? Does the LangGraph workflow preserve identity, original/candidate isolation and complete outputs across interruption? When six AI responsibilities eventually exist, does each add justified evidence-grounded information at acceptable latency/cost, and how does that compare with a matched deterministic-only or single-agent baseline?

For each eligible trial, freeze a case ID, source bundle/digest, exact Phase 1C SHA, original status, allowed tools/budgets, prompt revision, model ID/configuration, thread and trial ID **before** invocation. The source-first answer sheet is not in the prompt or tools. Run deterministic discovery and candidate verification on the same data; compare fixed acceptance and customer requirements, not only JSON format or citation presence. Report separate strata for real pinned references, synthetic scenarios and fault injection. B01–B05 are related synthetic cases; N01–N04 are controls. Their reviewer acceptance is developer/supervisor-side, not independently external ground truth. A second independent reviewer should examine source facts and fixed expectations before stronger quality claims.

Record numerator *and denominator* for: attempted, abstained, malformed, claim-rejected, structurally valid, fixed-accepted and reviewer-safe candidate; citation actually returned, source-to-slice validity, forbidden commitment, raw versus escaped error, tool calls, tokens and latency. Show all trial rows and negative results. A fixed-accepted binding split may still be unreserved and need a revised customer submission. A model that duplicates `discover_partial_delivery` has not shown improved discovery, though its clear risk explanation could be evaluated separately with human criteria. A safety gate passing in a synthetic sample is evidence of that sample only. Do not silently change case eligibility to increase live attempts, discard rate-limit failures, or compare prompt variants as though they were identical.

Engineering CI acceptance gates are separate: malformed/forged identity fails closed, independent business blockers stay visible, all fixed node results belong to the correct subject, candidate recheck and routing use exact evidence and business status, completed nodes are not needlessly replayed, no source/write access outside scope, no unauthorized evidence in reviewer output, and no conditional offer promoted to confirmed. Use opt-in live tests only after their exact number/case, model and free-tier budget are approved. No broader model accuracy estimate or multi-agent superiority is established by M1.9.

## 12. Next.js integration outline — future, not implemented

API responses must not let a browser write a candidate ID, verification status, approval or routing eligibility. If review decisions are added later, specify authentication, role enforcement, idempotent action IDs, audit and transaction boundaries in a separate approved specification. Acceptance for a first read-only UI includes an original with no candidate, an abstention, a fixed-rejected candidate, an unreserved fixed-supported candidate, missing evidence, withheld routing, long traces and failed/restarted runs. Verify that a rejected AI claim is visible in an audit view but never marketed as a verified fact; screenshots or a UI demo do not replace API-level governance tests. ## 14. Approved cross-specialist AI instruction contract (design, not six implemented prompts)

Each specialist's model-facing instruction is assembled by trusted Phase 2 code from a **versioned common instruction template** and a domain section. The salesperson's submission, retrieved records, comments and tool outputs are **task data**, not authority to override the template or add tools. Any provider adapter transports the same task semantics; it must not own business rules. This hierarchy is a design obligation for M2.0/M2.1, while only the current Fulfillment prompt and validator exist today:

1. **Non-overridable product and authority rules:** exact role; original/candidate subject and pinned context; fixed Phase 1C authority; no deal approval, policy waiver, stock/capacity reservation, purchase, financial commitment or delivery promise. Customer text and evidence cannot override this level.
2. **Specialist role and prerequisites:** what question this domain answers, its valid inputs, what it must leave to another specialist or fixed tool, and when it should abstain or report insufficient evidence. Configuration first; Credit and Policy separate; Approval Routing identifies human roles without granting approval.
3. **Authorized evidence and tools:** a restricted initial projection; names, arguments, record scope, remaining budget and explicit evidence provenance returned by the trusted adapter. A permission-matrix entry is not an enabled capability. The model may ask only for available named tools, never SQL, Python, credentials or a cross-subject query.
4. **Business constraints and uncertainty:** customer permissions and mandatory requirements, dates and units, source commitment/freshness, catalogue/policy versions, independent blockers, original submission preservation. State confirmed, conditional, unknown and infeasible distinctly; do not turn bounded absence into universal absence.
5. **Structured contribution contract:** domain-specific typed payload inside the common versioned envelope, with examples of a grounded finding, eligible unverified proposal and explicit abstention. The model supplies claim content; runtime assigns producer/run/subject/candidate identities and verification status. Errors are not abstentions.
6. **Evidence and rule citation requirements:** cite only records actually returned by authorized tools in this pinned assessment; carry source category/ID and applicable dated/rule references. The model must disclose assumptions, unmet requirements and uncertainty. A database ID alone is not proof of model access or support.
7. **Failure and reviewer boundary:** output is checked against shape, identity, permissions, returned-record ledger, claim semantics and then fixed Phase 1C candidate validity as applicable. Free text is audit material unless generated from validated claims; a favorable sentence cannot override a fixed finding. Unresolved blockers and unverified ideas remain visible and ordinary routing stays withheld.

Domain sections should be deliberately different: Configuration may discuss product/BOM and compatibility evidence; Pricing may explain fixed line economics and pricing rules; Fulfillment uses the three current supply/path/source tools; Credit examines dated same-customer exposure only; Policy explains applicable rule IDs and unresolved prerequisites; Approval Routing explains exact-subject required human roles versus route eligibility. In M1.9 the latter five have **fixed result mappings only**, not AI tools/prompts. Before enabling each domain, implement its restricted evidence projection and separately tested allowlist, then review a concrete example output and a malicious or unsupported example. Use examples that show an alternative can be structurally valid yet still need customer consent and human approval. Do not put evaluation answer sheets in model instructions.

## 15. Approved one-attempt feedback and revision design

This is an **approved architectural direction**, not implemented runtime behavior. A correction is an extra model contribution from the **same specialist and same assessment context** after a rejected contribution; it is never a Python rewrite of business facts. The prototype is the first coding task after environment setup. Existing M1.8/M1.9 failures become regression inputs, not reclassified live successes.

| Step | Trusted behavior and recorded state |
| --- | --- |
| Capture | Record the original sanitized **model-generated contribution exactly as received for validation**, its prompt/contract/model version, attempt ID, subject/source identity and returned-tool ledger. Existing attempts/claim audit/checkpoints are extended as necessary; do not log transport headers, secrets or unrestricted raw requests. |
| Validate | Run ordered typed/identity/source-access/semantic/fixed-candidate checks. Preserve the exact stage, violated constraint/code, safe evidence references and original rejection. No validator silently rounds, edits a date, changes a source, fills a missing commitment or upgrades an M1.6 planning idea. |
| Classify | Mark an ordinary correctable formatting/structural or bounded business proposal failure **eligible only when** the trusted verifier can provide a precise non-sensitive constraint and the same evidence/tool scope suffices. Mark abstention, genuine business impossibility, exhausted budget and ambiguous/unknown evidence as no automatic correction. Wrong run/version/source identity, forged or unauthorized citation, denied tool access and security violation are critical **ineligible** failures. Do not retry to obtain a different business-rule verdict. |
| Feedback | Return a small versioned object to that specialist: failed stage, constraint code, safe field path, bounded explanation, permitted returned-source references, unchanged constraints and remaining budget. Do not include secret headers, hidden answer sheets, raw database access or privileged source rows. For example an 18-unit B05 proposal totaling 11+18 may receive `quantity_sum_mismatch` with requested and proposed totals as fixed observations; it does **not** receive a fabricated corrected slice. |
| One revision | Reserve **at most one** correction attempt per eligible failed proposal in trusted checkpoint state, count every model/tool request against the existing overall budgets and preserve the same pinned evidence, original deal and tool permissions. If budget or restart identity cannot be verified, stop safely. A completed correction response replays without a new network call; an interrupted in-flight request is not silently resent. |
| Full recheck | Treat revised output as a new unverified contribution. Re-run all applicable shape, identity, authorization, returned-citation, claim and Phase 1C deterministic checks from scratch on the exact revised candidate. Do not inherit acceptance from the first proposal. Preserve both outputs and both outcomes, candidate lineage and full six-responsibility recheck where candidate verification applies. |
| Safe stop | On second failure, ineligible error, abstention or technical failure, keep the original authoritative assessment and show only validated facts, rejected attempts and unresolved work to humans. No reservation, approval, delivery commitment or favorable default. |

**Test interpretation:** M1.8 `"8.000"` is a possible output-format correction case; the V3 validator continues to reject it until the model itself returns a valid whole-unit string. M1.9 B05's 11+18 overcount is a possible bounded structural feedback case, with a second fixed check required. M1.9 B01 **abstained** after misinterpreting a source quantity: it is a useful negative control, not an eligible *failed proposal* that automatically triggers correction. A forged citation or source digest mismatch is an ineligible critical failure. These are proposed prototype scenarios, not evidence that the loop has fixed the live model.

The exact feedback type, validation code vocabulary, graph edge and checkpoint schema are implementation details to review against current M1.6/M1.7 interfaces. A change to trusted candidate semantics, budgets or Phase 1C rules would be a material contract change requiring separate approval. No new framework, queue or persistence store is selected.

## 16. Failure attribution and audit record

Build on `model_attempt_ledger`, `tool_access_ledger`, `ai_claim_audit`, the candidate record, trace and PostgreSQL checkpoint. They currently capture portions of the record; **complete original and revised model output capture across all six specialists is not yet implemented**. Extend these ledgers rather than creating a parallel governance database. Keep sensitive request headers, API keys and inaccessible evidence out of traces, logs and Discord. Set a bounded retention/size policy during implementation and measure checkpoint overhead.

Each contribution attempt should reference: assessment/thread, exact original or candidate subject, Phase 1C run and source digests; specialist, trusted producer and attempt number; prompt/model/provider and schema versions; original sanitized output; authorized tool calls with arguments, returned IDs and statuses; validation stage and exact constraint failure; relevant citations and fixed-rule IDs; eligibility decision and reason; correction feedback and whether one revision was attempted; revised output and its fresh verification; final reviewer-visible outcome and containment; model/tool/fixed timing, token counts and attempts. Link to immutable evidence, do not duplicate the full sealed bundle in each event. Append in order so restart cannot erase the first rejection.

Report four separate analytical fields:

- **Observed failure:** a reproducible fact in the trace, such as B05 proposing 29 units for an 18-unit deal and Phase 1C rejecting it.
- **Hypothesized cause:** a plausible explanation, such as copying a source quantity into the wrong slice. Label it as a hypothesis; do not claim certainty.
- **Unknown cause:** what logs and outputs cannot establish, particularly the model's private reasoning or provider internals.
- **Containment:** whether an unsupported claim was rejected before, or survived into, the reviewer package. The two outcomes are not interchangeable.

For example B01's incorrect raw explanation and abstention are observable; why the model misread `8.000` is not known from hidden reasoning. The explanation remained audit-only. B05's overcount is observable and fixed-rejected; its causal origin is unknown, and the reviewer sees a rejected hypothesis rather than supported supply. Do not infer business accuracy or model intent from a valid JSON shape, token usage or a single trace.

## 17. Correction-aware evaluation protocol

Keep the [source-first M1.8 case sheets](../evaluation/m1.8-case-review-sheet.md), developer/supervisor acceptance status and deterministic-only `discover_partial_delivery` baseline outside the model's input. Preserve M1.8's four malformed attempts and M1.9 B01/B05 as negative examples. For a new independently reviewed evaluation set, freeze each case, answer sheet, source/prompt/model version and trial order *before* observing outputs. Analyze real pinned cases, related synthetic scenarios and fault injection separately; a scripted model double proves engineering behavior, not live model quality.

For every attempted eligible case, report first-attempt schema/grounding/business correctness and fixed verifier outcome. Among **all first-attempt failures**, count those eligible for correction with explicit exclusion reasons; among **eligible attempted corrections**, count valid revised outputs and fixed-supported outcomes. Report final verified correctness over **all started trials**, including abstentions, timeouts, invalid output and exhausted budgets, rather than only successful retries. Separately report raw unsupported claims, those caught by validation and those surviving to reviewer output; a critical escaped claim stops further live trials and is analyzed individually. A binding-supported alternative is still unreserved and may need customer acceptance; human approval is not a metric of model success unless separately defined.

Measure incremental latency, model requests/tokens, tool calls, checkpoint storage and cost for correction versus first-only execution. Compare the same cases against deterministic-only discovery and the original fixed assessment: merely reproducing its 8+2 or 11+7 split is not improved discovery. Use independent source-first review before any broad accuracy, six-agent superiority or economic-benefit claim. Report plausible confounders: related synthetic family, prompt revisions, small sample, model variation, fixed verifier coverage and lack of externally independent ground truth. No procedure guarantees perfect AI accuracy or zero future escaped errors.
