This log records decisions for the Phase 2 implementation blueprint. It does not override the approved Phase 1B business architecture or Phase 1C data and deterministic rules. A provisional approval establishes direction; interface and acceptance details still require workshop review.

## D1 — First AI contribution: bounded Fulfillment alternative

- **Status:** Provisionally approved by Ali, 18 October 2026. D2 and D3 are now provisionally approved; exact contracts and lifecycle mechanisms remain in detailed design. No agent implementation is authorized by this entry.
- **Decision:** The first AI-assisted milestone focuses on the Fulfillment specialist proposing and explaining a bounded alternative to an original deal, followed by deterministic verification. Start with the narrow 20-unit partial-delivery scenario, while designing contracts to accommodate broader fulfillment scenarios later. The specialist may return **no suitable alternative** if evidence is insufficient or customer constraints cannot be met.
- **AI contribution:** Consider verified options, explicit customer constraints, tradeoffs, uncertainty and whether any suitable alternative exists. The model must do more than restate a candidate already discovered by Phase 1C. Its proposal is an unverified hypothesis until fixed tools check it. The system preserves the original deal and distinguishes confirmed, conditional, unknown and infeasible outcomes.
- **Authority:** Deterministic Python tools remain authoritative for quantities, availability, dates, business rules and candidate validity. AI cannot approve a deal, waive rules, reserve stock, book production, promise delivery or convert conditional supply into confirmed supply. A human reviews the result and decides whether to pursue a revision.
- **Rationale:** A constrained alternative demonstrates specialist proposal work, evidence use and governed rechecking while keeping the first implementation small enough to test and explain. Whether it improves on deterministic discovery is an evaluation hypothesis, not a result.
- **Alternatives considered:** (1) Explanation of fixed results only: simplest integration, but does not adequately demonstrate substantive specialist contribution. It can remain a fallback when no safe candidate exists. (2) Six AI specialists and general candidate search immediately: covers the target design sooner but depends on too many unsettled interfaces and failure paths. (3) Model-proposed split without fixed recheck: rejected because unsupported dates, quantities or commitments could reach human review.
- **Phase 1C dependencies:** Pin a compatible structured deal input, dated evidence reader, fixed decision/rule interface, frozen run and selected-source identity, trusted development fixture, and typed candidate/recheck interface. The partial-delivery candidate, reader-backed correlation and trace on draft PR #1 (`phase1c-candidate-contract`, audited at `90c0bc8e285ee69262c7db826b786096a77ad004`) are **development experiments**, not a released general contract. The Phase 2 branch (`phase2-agent-implementation`, audited at `c8ded2e5b9ec9e615280464aa0660f4a3eed8249`) has diverged; reuse requires an explicit, tested integration approach. Do not modify Phase 1C or merge PR #1 by implication. Full performance claims wait for independently accepted evaluation answers (0/36 at audit).
- **Implementation impact:** D2 must specify minimal agent-visible facts, permitted reads/tools, proposal and abstention outputs, citations, uncertainty and fixed-validation rejection. D3 must specify candidate identity, reruns, stopping, comparison and human handoff. Start with one narrow scenario; broaden only with tests and compatible Phase 1C interfaces.

## D2 — Versioned specialist contracts and bounded tool permissions

- **Status:** Provisionally approved by Ali, 18 October 2026. Exact typed fields and tool signatures require compatibility checks against the actual Phase 1C interfaces. D3 is now provisionally approved; this entry authorizes no agent coding.
- **Decision:** Use a common, versioned envelope for run/deal/candidate identity, frozen as-of/snapshot/catalogue/policy context, execution status, findings, evidence/rule references, uncertainty reasons and errors. Each of the six specialist responsibilities has a separate domain-specific input projection and output payload; implement these incrementally, without a universal agent framework.
- **Permissions:** Give each specialist only relevant evidence and named, authorized, read-only tools. Permit bounded investigation of selected source records and eligible options, not merely a precomputed answer. No unrestricted SQL, arbitrary Python, database credentials, mutation, reservation, booking, waiver, approval or other business-changing operations.
- **Authority separation:** Preserve distinct records for AI-generated findings/proposals, deterministic Python verification, and Orchestrator workflow-control decisions. All AI proposals start unverified. Fixed tools determine candidate validity, quantities, dates, evidence status and rule compliance. The Orchestrator has no business approval authority.
- **Uncertainty and failure:** Allow explicit abstention/no suitable alternative, missing-evidence and uncertainty reasons, and typed execution errors. Do not use uncalibrated numerical confidence scores. Preserve confirmed, conditional, unknown and infeasible evidence states, the original submission and evidence provenance.
- **Rationale:** A common envelope lets the Orchestrator reconcile runs and trace failures, while specialist payloads preserve distinct domain behavior. Bounded read-only investigation lets an agent contribute a proposal without taking authority over business facts or side effects.
- **Alternatives considered:** (1) Full database/SQL or arbitrary execution access: flexible, but weakens scope, reproducibility and control. (2) A fully precomputed answer to paraphrase: safe but insufficient for meaningful investigation. (3) One identical payload for every specialist: superficially simple but invites meaningless fields. (4) Numeric model confidence: no calibrated interpretation. The approved direction is named tools, separate payloads and explicit evidence/uncertainty.
- **Phase 1C dependencies:** Confirm real `read_run`, compiled-rule and candidate interfaces; source ID/freshness representations; frozen run identity; agent-visible input projection; output validator/schema. PR #1's candidate and runtime envelopes are development proofs and may change. Integration requires a pinned interface and combined PostgreSQL tests; the active Phase 1C draft is not implicitly merged. Hidden evaluation answers and reviewer drafts remain inaccessible to specialists.
- **Implementation impact:** Define exact typed envelopes, per-specialist payloads and allowlists; validate references against reader-selected source rows; reject or clearly quarantine malformed, mismatched or unsupported model claims. D3 must specify state transitions, retries, reruns and final human package. Phase 2 cannot label deterministic Phase 1C responsibility summaries as AI executions.

## D3 — LangGraph Orchestrator, lifecycle and safe failure

- **Status:** Provisionally approved by Ali, 18 October 2026. This supersedes the earlier recommendation to use plain Python as the primary M1 state machine. The detailed mechanisms listed below remain open; no agent coding is authorized by this documentation update.
- **Decision:** LangGraph is the selected orchestration framework from the beginning of Phase 2 implementation and supports the user's learning objective. Use a simple graph for M1 with typed versioned state, nodes, conditional edges, explicit dependencies and bounded candidate recheck loops. Ordinary Python services own business rules and deterministic checks. Specialist contracts remain independent of LangGraph internals. Expand graph branching, retries, checkpoints and resumption only as justified; no additional database, queue, vector store, tracing service or paid API is implied.
- **Configuration-first clarification:** Configuration validates first in the real flow using existing deterministic checks through a specialist interface. Fulfillment is the first AI-intensive specialist, investigating the 20-unit shortage. Pricing and all other specialists respect prerequisites. Configuration AI substitutions/corrections come later; this does not change Phase 1B's six responsibilities.
- **Run state and provenance:** Track original deal/run IDs and immutable submission reference; as-of timestamp, identifiable frozen evidence context, source fingerprint, catalogue/rule/policy versions; original or candidate subject; per-subject specialist and fixed-verification results; dependency/execution statuses; candidate lineage/diffs; trace, model/tool calls, retries/budgets, errors/conflicts/stops and final package status. Specialists return typed results; the Orchestrator validates them and controls transitions, never business approval. Keep AI claims, fixed verification and workflow-control decisions distinct.
- **Frozen evidence:** As-of time controls dated fact eligibility; a database transaction snapshot controls visible row versions; a recorded source fingerprint supports provenance/replay. A snapshot reference alone does not ensure consistency across graph nodes or transactions. Define and test a mechanism to retain or reconstruct identical selected evidence for original and candidate checks. Refresh requires a separately identified assessment context.
- **Dependencies:** Configuration precedes dependent Pricing/Fulfillment. Credit needs verified value. Policy waits for only the prerequisite results of applicable rules, keeping Credit and Policy separate. Routing waits for complete reconciled required findings and is recomputed for the exact checked original/candidate. Execute eligible M1 graph nodes sequentially; consider concurrency later only for independent nodes sharing the same frozen context, after measuring correctness, latency and traceability.
- **Candidate isolation:** Original submission/results are never overwritten. Each AI proposal begins unverified and has its own ID, original/parent linkage, precise changes, status, evidence, trace and specialist results. A human may later create a linked revised submission through a separately authorized process. Candidate structural validity is distinct from business feasibility, commitment and human approval.
- **M1 example and verification:** Original asks for 20 by Friday; fixed evidence supports 12 by Friday and a possible conditional eight later; customer permits partial delivery. Fulfillment AI may propose a split with tradeoffs or abstain. Fixed tools check line identity, quantities, groups/configuration, customer constraints, dates/slices, dated source IDs, freshness/commitment, rules and candidate context. M1 reruns all six deterministic responsibilities on the first candidate as a conservative baseline. Later selective reruns require proven matching fingerprints, evidence versions and dependencies; Routing always recomputes.
- **Safe failure:** Distinguish rule violations; missing/stale evidence; conditional/unknown findings; abstention; malformed output/invented references; rejected candidate; tool failure/model timeout; identity/version mismatch; conflicting findings; exhausted budgets. Never promote a gap/error to a favorable outcome. Reject unsupported claims, preserve the fixed original assessment if AI fails, retry only transient idempotent operations, never retry a real rule violation hoping for a pass, and surface unresolved conflict/critical unknown without an ordinary executable route. Record errors and stops.
- **Provisional M1 engineering defaults:** At most two distinct candidate proposals, one candidate revision level and one retry of an eligible transient model/tool failure. Add configurable, measured time and tool-call budgets in detailed design. Stop safely on hard blocks, critical unknowns, identity mismatch, exhausted budgets or no suitable alternative; prevent unbounded graph loops. These are engineering defaults, not business thresholds.
- **Human package:** Show original findings, every considered candidate and verification result, exact differences, unmet customer requirements, confirmed/conditional/unknown/infeasible states, dated source/freshness, missing commitments, applicable human roles/order, risks/conflicts, completion/stop reason and trace. This is a recommendation and review artifact, never approval or a business commitment.
- **Alternatives and rationale:** Plain Python as primary orchestration is simpler but was explicitly superseded by the user's LangGraph selection and learning objective; do not silently substitute it. Immediate concurrency and extensive checkpoint infrastructure add complexity without M1 evidence, so begin sequentially with a small graph. No recheck or model-only validation would weaken correctness; full M1 deterministic recheck is the approved baseline.
- **Dependencies/impact:** The exact LangGraph state schema, edges, checkpoint/persistence design, evidence snapshot/reconstruction mechanism, candidate lineage transitions, selective-rerun fingerprints, final budgets, failure taxonomy/recovery, Phase 1C integration and workflow acceptance tests remain open. Pin compatible Phase 1C reader/rule/candidate interfaces from draft PR #1 through an explicit integration process; do not alter Phase 1C or merge by implication. The Phase 2 architecture and roadmap must reflect LangGraph and Configuration-first execution before implementation.

## Workshop 2 — LangGraph detailed architecture directions (W2.1–W2.4)

**Status:** Approved architectural directions by Ali on 18 October 2026; exact types, signatures, persistence and integration mechanisms remain provisional. Full rationale, teaching example, edge cases and proposed acceptance matrix: [Workshop 2 technical specification](../architecture/workshop-2-langgraph-state-evidence-and-transitions.md).

- **W2.1 — Shared state:** One authoritative typed/versioned LangGraph assessment state; isolated domain-specific specialist input/output projections and optional private temporary agent/subgraph state. Original and candidate results remain separate. The Orchestrator validates outputs and controls state transitions; agents cannot overwrite other agents' findings. Chosen over separate persistent agent lifecycles to reduce synchronization complexity without losing specialist isolation.
- **W2.2 — Frozen evidence:** Pin the assessment's evidence/dataset version and as-of context, while permitting bounded discovery of further eligible records from that same context through authorized read-only tools. Log newly discovered evidence and prohibit silent version mixing. The exact Phase 1C-compatible replay/bundle mechanism is **open**; as-of timestamps and hashes alone do not reproduce PostgreSQL MVCC snapshots. Live ERP mutations are not the main M1 concern; thesis reproducibility is.
- **W2.3 — Checkpointing:** PostgreSQL-backed LangGraph checkpoints from M1; isolated thesis database/schema, no interference with Odoo/n8n, preserved run/evidence/candidate identities and budgets across restart. No Redis, new DB, queue or paid service implied. Exact checkpointer versions, migrations, retention and restore tests remain open. User can assist with VM setup when given explained safe commands.
- **W2.4 — Transitions:** Explicit rule-controlled LangGraph conditional edges, bounded candidate loops and safe stopping. After **every specialist**, validate schema, identity, provenance, evidence references and dependencies; then select the next eligible node. Perform separate overall reconciliation when required checks finish. Configuration first; Fulfillment first AI-intensive specialist. M1 candidate gets full six-responsibility deterministic recheck; routing only for fully reconciled subject. Business shortages may trigger alternatives, not automatic global stop.

**Alternatives:** W2.1 independent persistent agent states rejected for unnecessary coordination; W2.2 long-lived transaction across model calls rejected as fragile, exact replay/bundle choice pending; W2.3 no/in-memory checkpoints rejected in favor of durable PostgreSQL; W2.4 unrestricted LLM routing rejected in favor of explicit predicates. **No Phase 1C modification or agent coding authorized.** TG1 technology-change approval still applies.

- **Decision:** Define (1) restricted, versioned Fulfillment input; (2) separate typed AI proposal **or abstention** output; and (3) an authoritative deterministic verification result. Candidate IDs/lineage and verification statuses are assigned/controlled by the Orchestrator and fixed tools, never by model assertion.

### W3.4 — Bounded Fulfillment investigation operations

Validate model citations only against records returned to that specialist in this pinned context. ### W3.5 — Specialist-specific permission registry

- **Status:** Approved architectural direction by Ali, 19 October 2026. Exact entries, argument/result schemas and Phase 1C adapters remain provisional. No agent coding or new dependency is authorized.
- **Decision:** Use one small, versioned Python permission registry with six distinct specialist allowlists and parameter/record scopes, enforced on **every** named read-only tool call by the Phase 2 adapter. Specialist identity is supplied by the trusted Orchestrator's execution context, never accepted from model-generated arguments. No external authorization service or new library.

A disabled capability reports unsupported/unavailable capability, not an empty evidence result or fabricated answer.

- **Dependencies/impact:** Draft PR #1's `read_run` supplies a broad multi-domain bundle and fixed decision attribution, not ready-made six tool APIs. Phase 2 adapters must project and guard actual selected data without reimplementing Phase 1C rules. Test cross-specialist calls, wrong-customer/line/subject/version IDs, unavailable capability, hidden evaluation-answer access, and references to records never returned to the calling specialist. ### W3.6 — Shared specialist-result envelope and separated authority records

- **Status:** Approved architectural direction by Ali, 19 October 2026. Exact types, field names, encoding and Phase 1C mappings remain provisional. No agent code or new dependency is authorized.
- **Decision:** All six responsibilities return a concise common **versioned specialist-result envelope** plus their own typed domain payload. The envelope carries run/deal/original-or-candidate subject and frozen evidence identity, specialist/attempt, execution outcome, contribution outcome, cited source/rule references, uncertainty reasons and typed error information. It references authoritative evidence and tool/graph traces instead of duplicating the full LangGraph state.
- **Two independent outcomes:** Execution outcome distinguishes completed, failed and skipped invocations; contribution outcome for a completed invocation distinguishes a finding, proposal, abstention or no applicable contribution as appropriate. Successful execution does **not** imply a favorable business finding, verified proposal or permission to transition. A completed specialist may report an unknown or conditional business result; the Orchestrator still validates the envelope, checks prerequisites and evaluates fixed business gates before advancing.
- **Trusted producer identity:** The Orchestrator/runtime assigns and validates specialist identity, attempt and producer type (`deterministic` or `AI`) from the invoked node/service. The model cannot authoritatively label itself, impersonate another specialist, claim fixed verification, select a subject or increment retries. Store AI-generated claims separately from deterministic verification and Orchestrator workflow-control decisions.
- **Distinct states:** AI abstention is a valid completed contribution outcome; model/tool timeout or malformed output is technical failure; stale/missing evidence yields a domain unknown/needs-evidence finding; conditional supply remains conditional; authorization denial is a separate tool-access result. Never collapse these into one empty or successful state.
- **Rationale and alternatives:** Arbitrary dictionaries undermine identity/provenance checks; one enormous shared domain schema forces irrelevant fields; prose-only output cannot drive safe transitions. A shared header with domain-specific payloads and separate fixed/control records makes validation and audit possible without pretending all specialists behave identically.
