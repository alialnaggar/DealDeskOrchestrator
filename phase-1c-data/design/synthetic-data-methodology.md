# Phase 1C synthetic data methodology — as built and provisional

**Recorded 14 October 2026 for thesis writing.** This describes the implementation on `phase1c-candidate-contract`, not a finalized dataset or a claim about real enterprise deal frequencies. The private repository is the source of truth. Phase 1A literature, Phase 1B architecture, and Phase 1C synthetic data have separate purposes. This record should be updated whenever the generation contract, sources, cohort counts, or release decision changes.

## Research purpose and unit of analysis

The fictional enterprise technology provider supplies structured deal submissions and dated business evidence to exercise six responsibilities: configuration, pricing, fulfillment, credit, policy, and approval routing. Fixed rules calculate limits and identify unmet constraints. Future agents may propose evidence-backed alternatives for human review; neither the data nor the system records an automatic approval or a delivery promise.

The primary unit is a submitted **deal**, with one or more deal lines. A `deal_run` fixes a policy/catalogue version, an `as_of_at` cutoff, and a data snapshot reference. Operational facts are selected only if visible at that cutoff. The relational representation is the [24-table schema](../implementation/schema.sql); the [typed data contract](../implementation/phase1c_data_contract.yaml) and [column rules](../implementation/phase1c_column_rules.yaml) distinguish inputs, generated context, derived values, and evidence. The schema is unchanged by this methodology record.

| Relational fixtures | Catalogue, BOMs, customer and credit master, operational rows, submitted deals, and queued run rows. | Generated SQL/JSON files under `implementation/`; all IDs, prices, source references, and company facts are synthetic. | Independent evidence channels within the simulation | Dated credit/AR, stock, inbound, capacity, digital proof, and service context, supplied separately from the salesperson submission. | Independently **generated files**, not externally verified real-world documents. A public reference can motivate a band or a relationship; it does not validate an individual fictional SKU or a synthetic outcome. The 102 generated non-buildable sellable product costs are computed from each drawn list price and a category p50 target margin. This creates artificial margin concentration. The 18 buildable sellable products use the separate BOM-based cost draft. The read-only spread experiment in the calibration sheet has **not** been adopted.

## Case construction and temporal validity

The generator first establishes valid product, BOM, customer, rule, and operational contexts. It then introduces deliberate boundary and conflict cases rather than relying on a random draw to encounter rare failures. Examples include allocated or stale stock, confirmed versus offer-only or cancelled supply, constrained build capacity, digital full-term proof versus missing/partial proof, ineligible service regions, credit exposure, margin floors, policy clauses, and a V2 `requires_product` dependency pass/conflict. The dependency rule is scoped to the two V2 copies so it does not silently alter V1 pilot or historical deals.

A generated case keeps its requested customer date separate from any proposed later delivery slices. The reader checks dates, quantities, versions, stock allocation, shipping, capacities, and evidence at the run cutoff. Five generated DIGITAL-PROVED cases use a separate, SHA-256-pinned synthetic provider manifest matching pool identity, quantity, activation and full quoted term. The other digital cases cannot turn a deal-supplied reference or partial-term proof into a confirmed commitment. Five eligible service cases still have no dated committed appointment and remain conditional. These are rules within the simulation, not real supplier/provider/service confirmations.

## Reproducibility and validation

Generation inputs are versioned with code, YAML, SQL fixtures, and JSON manifests. Renderers expose `--check` where applicable; the generated-batch report stores SHA-256 hashes of its source files, SQL, and provider manifest. A rerun should produce byte-identical outputs from the same source commit and seed. Any deliberate change to a source, seed, schema, policy, or generator requires a new report and review; a hash proves identity, not realism or correctness.

The [PostgreSQL workflow](../../.github/workflows/phase1c-postgres.yml) applies the 24-table schema, checks isolated and connected rows, validates typed JSON/rule coverage, regenerates fixtures, reads dated evidence, executes the deterministic decision gate, and produces pilot, historical, aggregate generated-batch, and per-case diagnostic artifacts. Generated-case tests check 100 distinct IDs, reader-selected rows, applicable dependency pass/fail, full decisions, old cutoff visibility, and proof acceptance/rejection. Database fixtures used for those checks are loaded in disposable/rollback-only test contexts. Both push and draft PR runs passed at commit `5650899623f5fed359f2b1f4906853e52d8d5be5`; subsequent edits require their own run.

The per-case artifact records raw reader-visible IDs separately from IDs attributed in Availability findings. Twenty-two negative stock/supply cases have no shipping-option attribution; this does not mean there was no raw row. Nineteen of 47 attributed IDs occur in one case, and 67 cases reuse at least one attributed ID. This is a source-review queue and a dependence limitation, not evidence of independent sampling.

## Paper-writing boundary and release criteria

A paper can describe the *implemented method*, specify the seed/configuration, report achieved counts with the commit/run used, and evaluate whether fixed rules detect seeded conflicts. It should label public calibration anchors, invented parameters, synthetic evidence, observed deterministic decisions, and independently reviewed answers separately. It must not call the 400 historical decisions ground truth, claim 100 independent cases, report generated statuses as accuracy, say six AI agents have run, or imply that a shipping option/eligible region is a booking or promise.

Before a final data-release statement: complete six field-owner marks (product attributes; list price; standard cost; supplier calendar; compatibility condition; runtime output), decide the two calibration reviews (36/156 V1 mix and price/cost basis), review source provenance and achieved case distribution, independently author/review all 36 evaluation answers without exposing them to the system, and rerun reproducibility/row/reader/decision checks against the selected release commit. `agent_execution_log.output_json` stays open until actual agent execution; do not synthesize completed agent logs. The current flag is `ready_for_generation=false`; neither this documentation nor passing CI freezes calibration or approves a dataset.

**Suggested paper methods structure:** setting and schema; provenance/fictionalization; seeded master and historical generation; targeted conflict construction and dated evidence; cohort separation and answer review; deterministic validation; limitations and threats to validity. Cite this record's linked source/configuration files and the final release commit rather than copying old design-status statements.


### Development-source registry for later evaluation input authoring

In a rollback-only PostgreSQL test, 16 reference runs plus 38 connected pilot/dependency runs and 100 generated runs are read to create a sealed CI registry of selected stock, inbound, production-capacity and digital-capacity row IDs. The later evaluation-preparation caller can validate its hash and expected 154-run/152-deal scope before passing the union as an exclusion set. This is a concrete data-isolation control, not a 36-case evaluation set. It omits the 400 historical runs and shared catalogue/customer/policy/lane/service context, and cannot detect semantically duplicated sources with new IDs; those require additional review.

The measured registry has 49 inventory, 12 inbound, 14 production-capacity and 10 digital-capacity row IDs. The versioned `implementation/phase1c_development_source_exclusions.json` is compared with the PostgreSQL regeneration on every workflow run. It is a source-identity baseline only; historical and semantic overlap review remains separate.


The registry also pins 154 run and 152 deal identities. Future held-out packet authoring uses `prepare_heldout_review_input`, which requires the verified pinned manifest and rejects overlap in run, deal and the four selected operational source-ID classes before sealing an input-only packet. A separate rollback-only database fixture exercises this path but is not counted as an evaluation case; shared and historical source review and independent answers remain open.


The reader selects workshop production-capacity rows only for submissions with a make-to-order line. A stocked-only evaluation-preparation example had previously carried irrelevant global capacity IDs from development; the pinned-ID gate exposed this. The scoped reader passed current and historical PostgreSQL regressions, retained the same 154-run exclusion registry and generated outcome distribution, and let the separate input-only example clear the ID gate. This does not establish semantic independence or a reviewed answer.

## Separate evaluation input construction — 14 October 2026

The new `render_phase1c_evaluation_cases.py` authors 36 provisional cases directly from the declared V1 synthetic catalogue and the 18-archetype matrix (two variants each). It reads no development or historical submissions or operational ledgers. The design is deterministic scenario construction: variant quantities are 2 and 4 concurrent/physical units, ordinary quote multipliers are 0.97 and 0.94, and dates, allocations, credit headroom, capacities and receipt/proof validity are explicit functions of the case index and variant. Digital line quantity represents the full 12-month term, with the concurrent units stated separately. These settings are experimental design choices, not empirical draws or conflict-rate estimates.

Each case gets new customer/credit, run and deal identities and case-owned operational rows. Stock cases vary free, fully allocated and stale observations. Supplier cases distinguish offers, confirmed PO/receipts and cancellation. Build cases use declared BOM-001 context with separately constructed component stock, alternate receipt, fresh/stale workshop views or a receipt beyond the represented horizon. Digital cases use new binding pools with full-term, missing or partial-term proof in a separate simulated provider channel. Service cases state eligible/ineligible regional context without a booked appointment. Commercial cases exercise credit headroom, near-cost prices and legal-review clauses. The same author knows the development archetypes; these descriptions are authoring intent, pending independent verification that the complete cases realize it.

The five sellable SKUs, their prices/costs, BOMs/production requirements, location codes and compiled policy are shared model context. Operational records are loaded one case at a time in a separate disposable database so other evaluation cases cannot supply stock, capacity, credit or lanes. Typed row validation, both development/historical identity exclusion registries and two equal source-only reader exports check construction/replay. The source-definition file includes separate provider records, while the export keeps provider files distinct from the salesperson packet; only the controlled loader chooses them. A reviewer-only authoring manifest stores archetype intent and source hashes. The exported input index contains no archetype labels, findings or scores.

No expected deal outcome is assembled during authoring and no agent or rule-only evaluation score is produced. Independent reviewers must inspect all source facts, semantic overlap, condition realization and limitations before writing expected findings outside system-visible inputs. The authoring count is 36 inputs, the independent-answer count remains 0/36, and there is no released evaluation dataset. Distinct IDs and shared archetype coverage do not establish statistical independence or prevent implementation-informed case bias.

The first isolated PostgreSQL authoring run (38586442975, commit `edc3e752d74dec85b4774ab0308d5930261a866e`) passed: each of the 36 inputs produced two equal reader packets, passed row contracts and both identity exclusion checks, and selected no other case's operational IDs. `evaluation/provisional_packet_index.json` now pins the 36 payload/provider hashes and the source-definition digest `7dff0db52b976d377d346c2bb56de22bf38b8161458729397e718572576222d6`. Subsequent CI must reproduce this index from a new database. This pins provisional input content, not calibration or reviewed answers. The isolated source set is treated as complete fictional context; empty AR/payment arrays mean no invoices or payment history in that context. This closed-world assumption, the five shared sellable SKUs and known archetype design require disclosure and independent case/answer review. The review procedure is in the evaluation README, and no baseline or agent score is produced here.
